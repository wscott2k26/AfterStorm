# AfterStorm Competitive Gameplay Benchmark — 2026-08-14

## Purpose

Use current best-in-class gamified productivity, self-care, and behavior-change apps as a quality bar without copying their characters, layouts, art, terminology, progression systems, or branded mechanics.

The rule is simple: **borrow the successful product principle, then express it through AfterStorm's own storm-damaged neighborhood, restoration, residents, weather, and real-life quest loop.**

## Products reviewed

### Finch: Self-Care Pet
Observed App Store strengths:
- tiny real-life self-care actions feed a visible companion;
- immediate rewards make small actions feel meaningful;
- customization and personality create attachment;
- recurring seasonal content creates reasons to return;
- insights provide a second layer of long-term value;
- the tone stays supportive rather than punishing.

AfterStorm response:
- the attachment object is not one pet; it is **The Block plus its residents**;
- every small quest must visibly change weather, lighting, a place, a resident, or a collectible;
- missed days never damage previous restoration.

### Focus Friend
Observed App Store strengths:
- one extremely legible core loop: focus -> character works -> reward -> decorate;
- cozy character animation gives otherwise ordinary focus time emotional weight;
- rewards have an obvious use in customization;
- simplicity lowers activation friction.

AfterStorm response:
- preserve one equally legible loop: **choose a tiny quest -> do it -> confirm -> watch the world restore -> earn Sparks/unlocks -> choose what is next**;
- never let filters, menus, settings, or monetization obscure that loop;
- character and resident reactions should make completed quests feel witnessed.

### Forest
Observed App Store strengths:
- behavior creates a persistent visual landscape;
- unlockable species/soundscapes give long-term collection goals;
- historical progress becomes a visual artifact;
- focus sessions have a clear beginning and end.

AfterStorm response:
- persistent visual proof is the neighborhood itself;
- restored streets, lights, plants, homes, weather, residents, collectibles, and later districts become the historical artifact;
- avoid Forest's punitive 'wither on failure' pattern because AfterStorm never destroys earned progress.

### Habitica
Observed App Store strengths:
- real tasks produce game currency, experience, gear, pets, and progression;
- multiple task types make the system flexible;
- collectibles, parties, challenges, and events extend the loop.

AfterStorm response:
- Sparks and collectibles already provide the currency/reward layer;
- life areas and contextual quests provide flexibility without exposing a complicated RPG task database;
- avoid HP loss, punishment, and visually dense RPG bookkeeping;
- future social systems should be cooperative restoration rather than competitive guilt.

### Pokemon Sleep
Observed App Store strengths:
- a real-world behavior produces something delightful to discover later;
- collection and new-area discovery create long-term curiosity;
- the product can be checked briefly rather than demanding constant screen time.

AfterStorm response:
- some restoration rewards should feel discovered, not merely counted;
- residents, pets, signs, weather treatments, and later districts can provide surprise after real-life effort;
- AfterStorm should reward leaving the phone and completing life, not demand more screen time.

### Fabulous
Observed App Store strengths:
- large goals are broken into approachable daily steps;
- journeys give direction without requiring the user to plan everything;
- coaching and routines create continuity between sessions.

AfterStorm response:
- quests remain intentionally small and concrete;
- The Block provides the journey map visually;
- later 'Storm Forecast' planning can give direction without adding guilt or streak punishment.

## Launch gameplay quality bar

A successful AfterStorm completion should communicate this reward chain clearly:

1. **I chose something small enough to start.**
2. **I did something useful in real life.**
3. **The app acknowledged it immediately with sound/haptic/light.**
4. **Something in my world visibly changed.**
5. **I earned Sparks and progress.**
6. **If I crossed an unlock threshold, the new collectible/resident feels special.**
7. **I can immediately understand the next meaningful thing to do.**

If a user completes a quest and only sees a number increase, the experience fails this benchmark.

## Premium presentation quality bar

The strongest competitors use coherent character/world art and immediate feedback. AfterStorm's visual differentiation must be stronger:

- original textured storm atmosphere, not a flat dark gradient;
- storm -> clearing -> afterglow progression tied continuously to real restoration;
- dimensional adaptive glass rather than gray translucent cards;
- blue -> teal -> gold/rose lighting evolution;
- tactile button compression;
- meaningful haptic patterns for quest accepted, completion, restoration, and collectible unlock;
- layered ambience and restoration audio;
- Cinematic, Balanced, Calm, and Follow System controls;
- Reduce Motion and Reduce Transparency fallbacks that remain beautiful.

## What AfterStorm must NOT copy

- Finch's pet identity, art, terminology, seasonal characters, layouts, or reward presentation;
- Focus Friend's Bean, room-decoration presentation, wording, or timer layout;
- Forest's tree-growing visuals, species system, or withering punishment;
- Habitica's RPG stats, HP, combat, classes, pixel-art language, or punishment loop;
- Pokemon characters, collection framing, sleep mechanics, or area presentation;
- Fabulous journeys, coaching scripts, visual language, or branded terminology.

## AfterStorm's defensible twist

**Real life restores a living neighborhood.**

Instead of one pet, one tree, one room, or a spreadsheet-like RPG, AfterStorm turns small real-life wins into a cinematic place that visibly heals:

- buildings repair;
- lights return;
- rain and clouds clear;
- wet streets catch warmer reflections;
- plants return;
- residents and animals appear;
- collectibles become part of the world/avatar;
- later districts provide expansion without erasing The Block.

The emotional promise is not productivity for productivity's sake. It is: **rebuild your world by rebuilding your day.**

## Current build gate

Before expanding launch gameplay further, the current luxury pass must be visually accepted in Appetize. Do not add new progression rules immediately before that acceptance gate. First prove that the existing quest -> reward -> restoration loop feels premium and legible. Then use this benchmark to design the next retention/gameplay spec.
